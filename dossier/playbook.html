Triage & Incident Response Playbook

The repeatable procedure used for this case, structured on the NIST SP 800-61 lifecycle. This is the artefact the IR Lead defends in the walkthrough: given the alert, what we did, in what order, and why.

Owner: Sajay Chandran — IR Lead

Triage decision flow

When an insider-threat alert fires, the on-call analyst works top to bottom. The goal of triage is a fast, defensible true-positive / false-positive call and the right severity.

1 · ValidateIs the alert a true positive? Confirm the account, the actions and the data touched against raw logs.
2 · ClassifyInsider vs. compromised account? Check identity signals (geo, MFA, device).
3 · Score severityWhat data, how much, did it leave? Sensitive data + egress = P1.
4 · EscalateP1 → page IR Lead, open case, start evidence clock, notify Legal/HR.
5 · ContainDisable access, isolate host — only after evidence capture is agreed.
Decision gate — is this an insider or a hijacked account? If identity signals are clean (consistent geo, MFA satisfied on the enrolled device, known hardware) → treat as insider: preserve evidence, loop in HR/Legal, and avoid tipping off the subject. If signals are anomalous → treat as account compromise: force credential reset and hunt for the external foothold. This case went down the insider branch.

Response phases (NIST SP 800-61)

1 · Preparation (what was already in place)
  1. UBA baselining, DLP policies, EDR with tamper protection, SIEM log forwarding, device-control auditing.
  2. This IR playbook, an evidence locker, and named roles (IR Lead, forensics, DLP, UBA).
  3. Pre-agreed contacts for HR, Legal and CERT-In reporting.

Gap found later: no automatic "leaver watch" for staff under notice. Added as a recommendation.

2 · Detection & Analysis
  1. Critical UBA alert (UBA-3341) auto-correlated with DLP-ALR-5521 and paged the on-call analyst.
  2. Validated true positive: confirmed bulk reads, the privilege change, encrypted archiving and the upload against raw logs.
  3. Classified as insider using identity signals (see UBA).
  4. Built the timeline, extracted IOCs, mapped to ATT&CK.
  5. Scored severity P1 (sensitive IP + regulated personal data confirmed to have left).
3 · Containment

Short-term (executed 28 Sep 11:45 IST):

  1. Capture volatile evidence and agree the image plan before isolating (so nothing is lost).
  2. Disable the account; revoke all active sessions and tokens; revoke the VPN certificate.
  3. Network-isolate the laptop via EDR (keeps it powered for forensics but cut off from the network).
  4. Block the destination domains/IPs at proxy and firewall; block the USB device ID.
Why disable, not delete: the account and its mailbox are evidence. Disabling preserves them; deletion would destroy the record and tip off the subject.
4 · Eradication
  1. Remove the improper group membership and audit the restricted group for any other anomalies.
  2. Org-wide sweep for stale privileged memberships from past role changes; revoke what is unneeded.
  3. Confirm no persistence or secondary accounts were created (none found in scope).
  4. Add the four Sigma detections to the SIEM.
5 · Recovery
  1. Verify source repositories are intact (read-only access confirmed; no tampering).
  2. Move R&D USB policy to block; enable newly-registered-domain blocking for sensitive groups.
  3. Send a preservation / takedown request to the cloud-storage provider for the uploaded data.
  4. Return monitoring to normal; keep heightened watch on related accounts for 30 days.
6 · Post-Incident (lessons learned)
  1. Root cause agreed: stale privileged access (see case summary).
  2. Six recommendations issued with owners and due dates.
  3. Metrics recorded: 10-day dwell, 36 h 47 m to containment — targets set to cut both.
  4. Detections and this playbook updated so the next case is caught at the privilege-change step.

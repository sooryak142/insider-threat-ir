Executive summary

Shortly after employee E-2231 resigned to join a competitor, monitoring flagged a change in his behaviour: repeated late-night remote logins that did not match his six-month baseline. Investigation showed that his account held membership of an IT support group that should have been removed when he transferred out of IT Operations in 2024. That leftover access let him place his own account into a restricted R&D group he had no current business need for.

Over the following week the account performed bulk reads far above the department norm — across the formulation file share, a Phase II clinical-trial SharePoint site, and a full export of his own mailbox. The collected data was consolidated and compressed into large encrypted archives, which is what made the activity stand out: the endpoint agent reported an archiving tool producing password-protected volumes whose contents DLP could not inspect. Part of the data was then uploaded to a newly registered personal cloud service, and the full set was copied to a personal USB drive during a weekend office visit.

On the final night the subject attempted to interfere with monitoring — a service-control attempt against the DLP agent and a clearing of the local Windows Security log. Both were detected: tamper protection blocked the first, and the SIEM already held a forwarded copy of the logs, so no evidence was lost. The IR team declared a P1 incident, disabled the account, revoked its VPN certificate and isolated the laptop within the same morning.

Confidentiality

4.3 GB of restricted formulation IP and Phase II clinical-trial data (including participant personal data) left the controlled environment.

Integrity

No evidence of data being altered. Source repositories were read, not modified; file-share and SharePoint contents verified intact.

Availability

No outage. Business systems stayed up throughout; impact is confined to data confidentiality and regulatory exposure.

Business & regulatory impact

  • Intellectual property: formulation datasets represent multi-year R&D investment; loss to a competitor is the primary business risk.
  • Personal data (DPDP Act 2023): the Phase II data set includes clinical-trial participant identifiers, triggering a data-protection assessment and likely notification to the Data Protection Board and affected principals.
  • CERT-In (28 Apr 2022 Directions): unauthorised data exfiltration is a reportable incident within 6 hours of detection; reported on 27 Sep at 02:40 IST.
  • Legal / HR: evidence preserved to support employment action and potential civil/criminal referral; handled by Legal, not the IR team.

Threat actor attribution

Attribution is assessed as a malicious insider — a departing employee acting alone, with high confidence. The reasoning follows the evidence rather than assumption:

Line of evidenceWhat it showedPoints to
Authentication logsAll sessions used the subject's own valid credentials and MFA, from his home IP — no sign of a stolen or shared accountInsider, not a compromised account
TimingActivity began 2 days after resignation and ended on his last working day; concentrated in off-hoursDeparting-employee motive
Access pathSelf-service privilege grant using his own leftover IT group membership — no external foothold or malware deliveryAbuse of legitimate access
ScopeTargeted exactly the formulation + clinical-trial data relevant to the competitor's product lineDeliberate, informed selection
Anti-forensicsAttempts to disable DLP and clear logs show awareness of monitoring — consistent with a technically literate insiderIntent, not accident

Alternatives considered and rejected: account compromise (no anomalous geolocation, impossible-travel or credential-theft indicators); accidental over-collection (the archiving, encryption and anti-forensic steps are inconsistent with innocent activity); third-party collusion (no second account or external handler observed in scope — left open for HR/Legal follow-up).

Root cause

Primary

Stale privileged access. Group membership from a prior role was never revoked, defeating least-privilege and enabling self-escalation. A joiner-mover-leaver (JML) access review would have caught it.

Contributing

  • No heightened monitoring for employees under notice period.
  • USB policy for R&D was in audit mode, not block.
  • Newly registered destination domain was uncategorised and therefore allowed by the web proxy.
  • No approval workflow on changes to restricted security groups.
